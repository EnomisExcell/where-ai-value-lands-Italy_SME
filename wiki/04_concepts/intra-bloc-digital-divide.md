# The Intra-Bloc Digital Divide

[[digital-empires-tripartite]] partitions the AI world into three blocs — US market, China state, EU rights. [[brussels-effect-and-mirage]] notes the structural limits of EU regulatory imperialism in *external* high-risk state-controlled domains. A complementary limit, currently absent from the wiki, runs *inside* the EU bloc itself: **value created by EU-bloc industrial and digital policy concentrates where the digital core is mature, and bypasses regions where it is not, replicating the digital-empires asymmetry at sub-national scale**. The Brussels effect has an internal mirage that mirrors its external one.

## What it is

Transizione 5.0, Italy's flagship 2024–2025 AI and digital-investment incentive, closed in November 2025 with **€9.6 billion in supported investments**, of which **80% was absorbed by Northern Italy** (Lombardy alone exceeded €1 billion). The 35% SME share within that 80% concentrates in industrial districts of Lombardy, Veneto, Emilia-Romagna, Piedmont. The Italian South participated below its population share and well below its industrial-base share.

The pattern is not specific to one programme. It reflects a structural fact: state-coordinated industrial policy in the EU rights-bloc presupposes a digital core ([[digital-core]]) that firms can absorb funds into. Where the digital core is absent — where broadband is thinner, ICT specialists are scarcer, complementary capital is missing — public funds are formally available but practically inaccessible. The [[sme-broadband-firm-size-gap]] documents the firm-size dimension of this divide; the geographic dimension is parallel and similarly persistent.

The implication for [[digital-empires-tripartite]] is that the three-bloc partition hides large intra-bloc variance. Lombardy operates on a digital empire materially closer to the US substrate (cloud-mature firms, ICT-specialist concentration, venture and industrial-policy capital flowing in) than to Calabria, which operates closer to the EU periphery sub-bloc. The pattern recurs in Germany (Bavaria vs East), France (Île-de-France vs centre-rural), Spain (Catalonia / Madrid vs Andalusia). The Brussels regulatory umbrella covers both centres and peripheries; the value capture does not.

## Evidence

- Transizione 5.0 closing data: €9.6B mobilised, 80% Nord, Lombardy >€1B, 70% manufacturing, 35% SMEs.
- [[sme-broadband-firm-size-gap]]: ~27 pp small-vs-large broadband gap, persistent for 5 years across OECD — the connectivity layer of the digital core.
- [[ai-skill-shortage-as-diffusion-bottleneck]]: skills gap as the most-cited SME adoption barrier across G7, with sub-national variance.

## Tensions

Extends [[brussels-effect-and-mirage]] rather than contradicting it. Where the existing page locates the mirage in EU regulatory ambitions over Russia and China, this concept locates a second, smaller mirage inside the EU itself — invisible at the bloc level, structurally significant at the operator level for any firm choosing where to deploy AI services in the EU.

## Open questions

- Does the intra-bloc digital divide narrow under EU-level cohesion policy, or does it widen as AI infrastructure consolidates in already-mature regions ([[sovereign-ai]] AI factories siting decisions)?
- Is the divide a *bloc-level* phenomenon (EU-specific because of cohesion-policy asymmetries) or a *general* sub-national pattern that also operates inside the US (coastal vs Rust Belt vs South) and China (coastal vs interior)?
