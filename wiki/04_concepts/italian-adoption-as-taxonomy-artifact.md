# Italian AI Adoption Gap as Taxonomy Artefact

Italy holds the widest large-to-small firm AI adoption ratio in the G7 at **4.7×** ([[g7-sme-large-firm-ai-adoption-ratio]]). The standard reading treats this as a measure of Italian SME weakness in adoption capacity. A second reading is structurally stronger: **the 4.7× is partly an artefact of measuring Italian reality through a US-centric adopter taxonomy that does not include the modal Italian archetype**.

## What it is

The [[oecd-sme-adopter-taxonomy]] uses four quadrants (Novices, Optimisers, Explorers, Champions) calibrated on the US/UK AI-supplier-and-tech-SME population. The wiki's open-question file already flags ([[05_open-questions]] §"Business archetype taxonomy") that a **sixteenth archetype is missing**: the *traditional-industry AI integrator* — the typical North Italian family business that adopts AI deeply inside core production processes without becoming an AI supplier, without crossing into Champion-class enterprise architecture, and without leaving the manufacturing / distribution sector it has operated in for generations.

Under the existing four-quadrant taxonomy, this archetype is invisible. Such a firm gets classified as an Optimiser if it has multiple off-the-shelf tools, or as an Explorer in narrow agentic use, but neither classification captures what the firm *actually is* — a vertically-integrated manufacturer with AI threaded into its operational core, not as a product but as production infrastructure. The single existing wiki Italian SME case ([[sme-optimiser-cross-functional-stack]], Brescia bag manufacturer) sits in exactly this misclassification: tagged Optimiser, but functionally closer to an SME-scale [[vertical-ai-orchestration]] than to the Sèvres bakery the OECD groups it with.

When a country's modal SME archetype is absent from the framework, that country reads as "underperforming" — even where adoption is genuinely happening, but in a form the framework does not count. The Italian 4.7× is therefore measuring **two things simultaneously**: a real adoption gap *and* a framework-Italy fit gap. Without disentangling the two, policy prescriptions risk pushing Italian SMEs toward Champion-style architecture (the US-aligned endpoint) when the optimal local trajectory is the missing 16th archetype.

## Evidence

- OECD G7 paper: Italy 4.7× large/small ratio, widest in G7 ([[oecd-sme-ai-adoption-2025]], [[g7-sme-large-firm-ai-adoption-ratio]]).
- Polimi 2025: 71% large firms / 15% medium / 7% small Italian adoption ([[osservatorio-polimi-ai-italia-2025]]).
- Wiki self-flag: the sixteenth-archetype gap is already named on [[05_open-questions]] §"Business archetype taxonomy" but has not been instantiated as a wiki concept page.

## Tensions

This concept does not deny that Italian SMEs face real structural barriers ([[sme-ai-finance-gap]], [[sme-broadband-firm-size-gap]], [[ai-skill-shortage-as-diffusion-bottleneck]]). It adds a measurement-bias layer on top: the *visible* gap mixes real barriers with framework-fit mismatch, in unknown proportions. Disentangling the two requires primary research on Italian district-firms that adopt AI inside core production — none currently exists in the wiki at named-firm granularity beyond the Brescia case.

## Open questions

- What fraction of the 4.7× ratio is genuine adoption gap versus framework-fit artefact?
- Does the same artefact recur in Germany (Mittelstand), France (entreprises de taille intermédiaire), Japan (chusho-kigyo)?
