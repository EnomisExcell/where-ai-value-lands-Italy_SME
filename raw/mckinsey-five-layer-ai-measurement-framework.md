## Why this matters to the research

The **operational counterpart** to the MIT NANDA 95% pilot failure finding ([[mit-nanda-state-of-ai-business-2025]] stub in the same PR). MIT NANDA documents that systematic monitoring correlates with significantly better outcomes; McKinsey proposes the **five-layer measurement framework** that operationalises what "systematic monitoring" actually means in enterprise practice. The two sources together convert the bear-floor failure-rate finding into a constructive operator-side prescription: failure is upstream-attributable to absence of layered measurement, and layered measurement is the dependent variable that distinguishes the 5% from the 95%.

Three structural contributions:

- **A measurement vocabulary** beyond the activity-vs-outcome dichotomy that dominates practitioner content. The five layers separate technical telemetry, operational KPIs, business KPIs, financial impact, and strategic outcomes — letting different stakeholders (ML engineers, function leads, CFO, CEO) measure at the layer that matters to them.
- **An "activity vs outcome" anti-pattern.** McKinsey explicitly flags the failure mode of measuring AI outputs (drafted proposals, generated code, summarised documents) rather than business outcomes (sales-cycle duration, win rate, time-to-close). Directly relevant to the contributor's daily-thought sustained-ROI lens.
- **A scaling definition.** A deployment is considered *scaled* only when AI shifts from initiative to business-as-usual, with sustained adoption and structurally improved operational KPIs. This is the operationalisation [[scaling-gap]] currently lacks — the wiki's scaling-gap page documents the 74%/16% artifact without specifying what counts as "scaled."
