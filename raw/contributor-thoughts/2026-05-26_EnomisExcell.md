# AI services for Italian SMEs and large-enterprise team workflows — the missing sustained-ROI lens

The wiki has KPI deltas and macro projections, but the unit of analysis that matters to an operator buying or building AI services in the Italian market is missing: **how long does the positive cost/benefit balance hold after deployment**. Specifically, does it survive five consecutive months — long enough to outlast the [[ai-productivity-firm-level]] J-curve and the first re-org pass.

## Threads

1. **The "decidedly positive over five months" criterion is the operator-side filter the wiki does not yet name.** Most documented cases — [[eri-use-case-deltas]] (BMW 30–40%, SBB 60%, Merck €100k+, drone >50%), the [[ai-leader-productivity-delta]] cohort (4.5% / 2.4× / 15%) — are *snapshots*. They do not answer the buyer's actual question: at what month does the curve cross into net positive, and does it stay there. The data page itself flags the figures as second-hand and unverified against originating company reports. The [[scaling-gap]] (74% / 16%) is the only systematically negative datum on sustainability, but it is silent on *why* and on *firm size*.

2. **Italian SMEs are the worst-served segment for sustained-ROI evidence.** [[oecd-sme-ai-adoption-2025]] documents Italy's 4.7× large/small adoption ratio — the widest in the G7 — and the +240% productivity-quantile gap, but a single representative SME case ([[sme-optimiser-cross-functional-stack]], the Brescia bag manufacturer). The structural counter-pressures the wiki already names — [[sme-ai-finance-gap]] (credit-tightening pushing SMEs toward short-term lending, "the wrong shape for multi-year AI integration"), [[sme-broadband-firm-size-gap]] (flat ~27 pp for five years), [[ai-skill-shortage-as-diffusion-bottleneck]] — together predict that the *typical* Italian SME deployment will *not* be sustained-positive at five months. The interesting question is which sub-population, in which sector (distretti industriali), with which complementary capital, beats that prediction.

3. **Large-enterprise team workflows in knowledge work are a wiki blind spot.** [[eri-use-case-deltas]] is heavy industry (energy, manufacturing, drone, materials). The wiki has no case on a finance team's controlling workflow at month six post-Copilot rollout, on a legal team's contract review six months after a domain-specialised assistant, on a sales-ops team's pipeline hygiene six months after a Clari/Gong-class integration. These are exactly the deployments an operator selling AI services into Italian large enterprises will be asked to justify.

4. **This intersects an open question already on the wiki.** `wiki/01_paper-planning/05_open-questions.md` §"Business archetype taxonomy" flags a missing 16th archetype: **"traditional-industry AI integrator"** — the type of firm the author is and the type of firm reading the paper. The Italian operator-side view, instantiated as a research lens rather than a personal essay, is what would materialize that archetype.

## What I am not sure about

- Whether "five months" is the right horizon, or whether the canonical [[ai-productivity-firm-level]] J-curve pivot is six-to-eighteen months — needs the McElheran (2024) primary read.
- Whether the right level of abstraction is sector × archetype × month, or whether anonymised vendor case studies (Microsoft / OpenAI / Anthropic enterprise pages) are the realistic data source given primary-source scarcity.
- Whether the [[sme-ai-finance-gap]] short-term-lending pressure means sustained-ROI deployments require either grant/subsidy support (the Brescia case received government support) or sit only at the Champion end — leaving the Novice / Optimiser core unservable by sustainable AI services.
