# Research dossier — sustained-ROI evidence for AI services in Italian SMEs and large-enterprise team workflows

Consolidates the findings from the `/discover` pass that followed the daily thought of 2026-05-26 (`raw/contributor-thoughts/2026-05-26_EnomisExcell.md`). The lens: documented AI applications where the cost/benefit balance is decidedly positive over a sustained period (~5 months), filtered to two segments — Italian SME CEOs as buyers, and team workflows inside large enterprises. Sources span the bear-side empirical floor (failure rates) and the bull-side success cases (Stanford, Vanguard, Anthropic Economic Index).

## The headline finding

**The evidence overwhelmingly supports the bear side on average sustainability, with a thin bull tail concentrated in specific configurations.**

- **MIT NANDA 2025: 95% of GenAI pilot programs fail to produce measurable financial impact.** Surfaced via S&P Global; primary source linked in the Kind 1 stub `raw/mit-nanda-state-of-ai-business-2025.md`.
- **MIT + RAND: 70–85% of AI initiatives miss expected outcomes. 42% of companies abandoned most AI initiatives in 2025, up from 17% in 2024.**
- **Practitioner consensus on payback timeline: 2–4 years for sustained ROI**, not five months. The contributor's "5-month" criterion is materially tighter than the industry expectation — and that is the operator-side point. Deployments that beat it are the ones worth selling.
- **Even where measurement is attempted, it often isn't.** Polimi Osservatorio 2025: *only 54% of large Italian firms using GenAI attempt to measure benefits.* The Italian sub-population is therefore largely unmeasured, which means absence-of-evidence is structural, not sampling.

## What is positively documented (the bull tail)

1. **Vanguard Group: ~$500M sustained AI ROI** across call-center support, personalised adviser summaries, and a **25% improvement in programming productivity**. Multi-use-case, multi-year. (Source: MIT Sloan coverage.)
2. **Forrester TEI Microsoft 365 Copilot, March 2025: 116% ROI, $19.7M NPV, $36.8M benefits vs $17.1M costs over three years** for a composite organisation. Important caveat: *projected* economic impact based on 8 early interviewees, **not measured returns from full deployments**. The samexpert.com counter-piece flags this gap directly. Treat as upper-bound illustrative, not benchmark.
3. **Arxiv "Intuition to Evidence" (2509.19708, 2025): developer-productivity longitudinal cohort study showing 4% engagement in month 1 → 83% peak by month 6 → stabilising at 60%. 31.8% reduction in PR review cycle time. Top adopters: 61% increase in code volume pushed to production, ~30–40% of code shipped contributing 28% overall increase.** The fetch returned only metadata (binary PDF); the secondary summary is the empirical anchor pending direct read.
4. **Sirion.ai legal-ops aggregated practitioner data: most legal AI implementations reach positive ROI within 1–3 months for simple use cases, 6 months for complex deployments.** Concrete example: Orangetheory Fitness contract-review time from manual → 30 minutes per document, an 80% improvement. *This is the cleanest evidence in the entire pass that the contributor's 5-month criterion is achievable, in this specific function (legal contract review).*
5. **Finance / HR consulting case: 1,500+ hours/month saved at a global consulting firm via compliance-reporting automation; up to 40% faster hiring cycles, 50% reduction in reporting time.** Aggregated practitioner data, no single-firm time-series.
6. **Stanford "Enterprise AI Playbook: Lessons from 51 Successful Deployments" (Pereira / Graylin / Brynjolfsson, March 2026):** the structural inverse of the MIT NANDA null sample. Primary research; PDF on Stanford Digital Economy Lab. Kind 1 stub in `raw/stanford-enterprise-ai-playbook-2026.md` for ingest by the maintainer.
7. **Anthropic Economic Index, March 2026 ("Learning Curves"):** users active on Claude for 6+ months have a 10% higher success rate in conversations, after controlling for task type. **First empirical evidence in this pass that *individual* sustained use compounds.** Source: anthropic.com/research/economic-index-march-2026-report.

## The Italian picture

Quantitatively confirms the OECD G7 framing and sharpens it:

- **Polimi Osservatorio 2025: market €1.8B (+50% YoY). 71% large firms / 15% medium / 7% small.** Manufacturing + retail driving growth; mature sectors decelerating.
- **Minsait "Artificial Intelligence in Italy 2025": 63% of large Italian companies adopting or planning AI; potential aggregate productivity uplift of €115B; current +3.2% aggregate productivity gain, +4.3% projected at 18–24 months.**
- **Confartigianato / OpenAI / Tom's Hardware reporting: Italian SME adoption 22% vs European average 32%; 3 out of 4 Italian SMEs do not invest in AI and have no plans to.** Confirms the OECD 4.7× ratio at the qualitative narrative level.
- **Practitioner secondary syntheses (DigiAi, Sammarco, Maia Management, Noratech): one cited case of a Milan manufacturing SME reaching −40% operating costs in 8 months.** Single secondary citation, no primary attribution found in the pass. Treat as illustrative pending primary verification.

The structural conclusion: **for the typical Italian SME, the 95% failure rate is the prior. The ones that beat it are concentrated in (a) manufacturing with embedded process measurement, (b) Champion-archetype SMEs with grant or sector-policy support (Transition 5.0, Competence Centres), (c) digitally-mature retailers leveraging platform-bundled AI (Shopify-class, Microsoft 365 Copilot-class).**

## Operator implications for an Italian AI services play

1. **Sell against the J-curve, not against headline KPIs.** The differentiator is not "AI gives you 30% productivity" — every consultancy now says that. The differentiator is "we instrument month-by-month so you can kill it at month 4 if it's the wrong fit, and you keep it past month 6 only if the curve actually crossed positive." MIT NANDA / RAND give the empirical permission for that framing.
2. **The most defensible Italian sub-segment is mid-to-large SMEs (15% adoption tier) with prior digital maturity in manufacturing or retail.** Below 15% adoption (small firms, 7%), the connectivity / skills / finance gaps documented in [[oecd-sme-enabler-quartet]] make sustained ROI structurally unlikely without grant support. Brescia case (in [[sme-optimiser-cross-functional-stack]]) is representative: government support was load-bearing.
3. **For large-enterprise team workflows, legal-contract-review is the function with the cleanest sustained-ROI evidence base (1–3 months simple, 6 months complex per Sirion practitioner data).** Finance / FP&A is second-cleanest (Vanguard, Juniper, generic consulting). HR / talent acquisition is third. General "Copilot for all knowledge workers" is the function with the *weakest* longitudinal evidence — Forrester's TEI is the canonical reference and it is projection-based, not measurement-based, with significant practitioner skepticism (samexpert.com).
4. **Italian deployments are systematically unmeasured.** Polimi: only 54% of large firms even try. That is itself a service opportunity — *instrumentation and longitudinal measurement as a deliverable* is differentiable in the Italian market where most competitors deliver only the deployment.
