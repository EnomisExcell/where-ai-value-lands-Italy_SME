# MIT NANDA — The GenAI Divide: State of AI in Business 2025

**Author(s):** MIT NANDA initiative (Networked Agents and Decentralized AI), lead researchers TBD on primary verification
**Year:** 2025
**Venue:** MIT NANDA research initiative; widely cited via secondary outlets (S&P Global, Fortune, VentureBeat, Forbes) in Aug–Oct 2025
**URL:** Primary report PDF: https://mlq.ai/media/quarterly_decks/v0.1_State_of_AI_in_Business_2025_Report.pdf — verified 2026-05-27. Title on cover: *"The GenAI Divide: State of AI in Business 2025."* Secondary coverage: Fortune (18 Aug 2025), legal.io, softwareseni.com analyses.
**Primary or secondary:** Primary report. Methodology: 52 executive interviews + 153 leader surveys + analysis of 300 public AI deployments.

## Why this matters to the research

This is the **single most quantitatively damning data point** the wiki currently lacks on the sustainability side of enterprise AI deployment. NANDA reports that **95% of generative AI pilot programs fail to produce measurable financial impact** despite $30–40B in cumulative enterprise investment. Combined with RAND's complementary finding that **70–85% of AI initiatives miss expected outcomes** and the **2025 abandonment rate of 42% (up from 17% in 2024)**, this constitutes the load-bearing empirical floor under the bear-case sustainability argument.

## Key findings (from primary report)

- **95% of pilots delivered no measurable P&L impact** (52 exec interviews + 153 leader surveys + 300 public deployments).
- **Vendor solutions succeed ~67% of the time vs ~33% for internal builds** — relationship-mediated measurement is itself causally upstream of success.
- **Most successful deployments allocate 50–70% of timeline and budget to data readiness** (extraction, normalization, governance metadata, quality dashboards) — directly answers the OECD's "AI-enabling inputs" enabler with an operational percentage.
- **Organizations with systematic monitoring report significantly better outcomes** than those with ad-hoc review — the empirical case that the measurement gap (Polimi: 46% of large Italian GenAI-users do not measure) is causally upstream of the failure rate, not just correlated with it.

For [[H2_u-curve-of-value]] it complicates the "bottom-right of the U" framing by removing the assumption that AI deployments quietly compound at the application layer — most never reach measurable financial impact at all. For [[scaling-gap]] it provides a *causal* refinement: the 74% / 16% BCG / Accenture figure measures inability to scale; NANDA measures inability to produce *any* measurable value. The two compound. For the contributor's operator-side research frame (`raw/contributor-thoughts/2026-05-26_EnomisExcell.md`), the 95% rate is the headline justification for a sustained-ROI-evidence lens.
